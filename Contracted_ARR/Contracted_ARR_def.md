# Contracted Annual Recurring Revenue

## Definition

Contracted Annual Recurring Revenue (CARR) is contracted annual recurring revenue, whether in production or not yet in production.

CARR differs from Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR), which has a distinct calculation approach. The two terms should not be used interchangeably.

Committed ARR is a term that is used synonymously with Contracted ARR (CARR).

## Business Value and Insights

Contracted ARR is one of the top metrics for a SaaS business to monitor. Change in CARR growth provides the clearest visibility into the health of a SaaS business.

Investors evaluating both private and public SaaS companies apply an Enterprise Value:Revenue multiple premium based on the growth rates of Contracted ARR, ARR, and GAAP revenue.

An important facet of CARR is that it is a measure at a point in time—much like a Balance Sheet item, as opposed to an Income Statement metric like Revenue, which is calculated over a period of time. CARR can be provided at any moment, whereas Revenue metrics require specification of a year, quarter, etc.

## Calculation Formula

$$\text{CARR} = (\text{MRR}^1 \times 12) + \text{Contracted but not yet recognized ARR}^2$$

<sup>1</sup> MRR is the most recent recognized monthly recurring revenue from subscriptions

<sup>2</sup> Not yet recognized ARR is contracted but not yet in production and/or recognized as GAAP Revenue—thus not recognized in current period MRR

### Data Inputs Required

Data Input #1: Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) associated with a subscription from last month.

Data Input #2: ARR contracted but not yet in production.

### Calculation Timing

Contracted ARR should be calculated monthly, or at a minimum quarterly.

Evaluating CARR trends between fiscal accounting periods, including monthly, quarterly and annually is critical to calculating “period over period” CARR growth rates.

## Nuances to Consider

### Nuance #1: Bookings vs CARR

The terms Bookings or New Bookings typically encompass total Contracted ARR from multi-year agreements, professional services revenue, and other non-recurring commitments. Bookings lack a standardized definition and should not be considered equivalent to Contracted ARR.

CARR should only reflect Contracted Annual Recurring Revenue and exclude non-recurring revenue such as professional services or variable usage such as one-time overage revenue.

### Nuance #2: CARR in multi-year agreements

For multi-year agreements, the contribution to CARR should represent only the contracted subscription revenue for one year forward.

When multi-year agreements include escalating annual subscription value each year, CARR must be precise and align with the exact agreement terms regarding the subscription value contracted for each subscription year. Only the amount of contracted ARR for each subscription year should be attributed to CARR.

### Nuance #3: Usage-Based Pricing impact on CARR

The revenue associated with usage based pricing that exceeds the annual contracted subscription amount should NOT be part of Contracted ARR—regardless of the overage being invoiced.

Invoiced amounts exceeding the subscription agreement value should not be considered part of CARR.

When no contracted minimum commitment (subscription) exists for usage-based pricing utilization models, no CARR is present.

Companies that primarily use a Usage-Based Pricing model without a subscription minimum annual commitment will find less value in the CARR metric.

### Nuance #4: New or Net New ARR vs Contracted ARR

Contracted ARR includes New ARR, Net New ARR and existing Contracted ARR.

New ARR examines only the Contracted ARR from “new logos.”

Net New ARR = New Name Customer ARR + Expansion ARR − Churn ARR − Downsell ARR. Expansion and Downsell ARR represent incremental changes from previous contracted ARR.

### Nuance #5: When to include churned and down-sell ARR in Contracted ARR

CARR should be reduced by churned ARR in the accounting period the down-sell or churn contractually occurs. Churned ARR should not be deducted upon learning of known future churn. Churned ARR should be reduced in the same period the contract expires and/or the agreement is not renewed.

## Sample Calculations

### Sample Calculation #1: Monthly Calculation at end of September, 2022

#### List of Input Values

MRR (Sep 22'): $1,500,000
Contracted ARR not in production by September 30, 2022: $400,000

#### Calculation Formula

$$(\$1{,}500{,}000 \times 12) + \$400{,}000 = \$18{,}200{,}000 \text{ CARR}$$

### Sample Calculation #2: Annual Calculation at the end of Fiscal Year 22'

#### List of Input Values

MRR (Dec 22'): $2,000,000
Contracted ARR not in production by December 31, 2022: $500,000

#### Calculation Formula

$$(\$2{,}000{,}000 \times 12) + \$500{,}000 = \$24{,}500{,}000 \text{ Contracted ARR (CARR)}$$
