# CAC Payback Period

*Alternative Name: Customer Acquisition Cost Payback Period*

## Definition

The CAC Payback Period is the number of months it takes for the gross profits from new customer ARR to pay back the sales and marketing expenses required to acquire that customer. Gross Profit equals New ARR × Subscription Gross Margin.

A key distinction from the CAC Ratio is that the CAC Payback Period considers gross margin and expresses the result in months rather than as a ratio.

## Business Value and Insights

The metric functions as a working capital measurement, helping organizations understand investment recovery timeframes after accounting for Cost of Goods Sold when executing growth strategies focused on new CARR acquisition.

CAC Payback Periods correlate with new customer value as measured by CARR per customer. While some use “average annual contract value” terminology, the standard recommends CARR to maintain consistency in capturing recurring revenue only.

## Calculation Formula

$$\frac{\text{Fully Loaded Sales and Marketing Expenses to Acquire New Customers}}{\text{CARR}^1 \text{ from New Customers} \times \text{Subscription Gross Margin Percentage}} \times 12$$

<sup>1</sup> CARR is Contracted Annual Recurring Revenue

The Marketing and Sales Expenses should be measured for the time period preceding the new ARR by the length of the sales cycle. For a ~90-day sales cycle, calculate Q2's metric using Q1 Sales & Marketing expenses divided by Q2 New ARR. For a ~30-day sales cycle, use the previous month's Sales & Marketing divided by the current month's New ARR.

### Data Inputs Required

Data Input #1: Fully Loaded Sales and Marketing Expenses. This data is typically available from the Income Statement.

Data Input #2: CARR from New Customers. This information typically resides in Customer Relationship Management Software or billing systems.

Data Input #3: Gross Margin Percentage. This is available from the Income Statement or Accounting System.

### Calculation Timing

The metric can be calculated using data from trailing 1, 3, 6, or 12-month periods.

## Nuances to Consider

### Nuance #1: Fully Loaded Sales and Marketing Expenses

Sales and Marketing expenses should be fully loaded including variable compensation, bonuses, benefits and any other shared expenses that are allocated to the departments. Sales commissions should be fully burdened up-front.

### Nuance #2: Timing of Sales Commissions

When sales commissions represent a meaningful percentage of CAC, accuracy improves by including commission expenses in the same period the new CARR is booked. This differs from current ASC 606 Income Statement guidance.

### Nuance #3: Customer Success Expense

Customer Success expenses should be included when personnel allocate time to new customer acquisition. The factored amount should reflect the time allocation percentage. Refer to your company's allocation of Customer Success to Operating Expenses versus Cost of Goods Sold for guidance.

### Nuance #4: Time Period of Expenses Versus CARR

The Marketing and Sales Expenses should be measured for the time period preceding the new CARR by the length of the sales cycle.

Example #1: For a ~90-day sales cycle, calculate Q2 using Q1 Sales & Marketing expenses divided by Q2 New CARR.

Example #2: For a ~30-day sales cycle, use the previous month's Sales & Marketing divided by the current month's New CARR.

### Nuance #5: PLG Business

Freemium or Free Trial expenses, including delivery costs like hosting and product costs, may be considered part of Customer Acquisition Cost.

### Nuance #6: Paid Pilots or Trial

When using paid pilots or trials, evaluate whether to include or exclude the associated expenses and revenue from the CAC calculation.

### Nuance #7: Usage-Based Pricing

For usage-based pricing generating predictable, consistent “variable annual recurring revenue,” the estimated variable ARR per customer could be added to Contracted ARR in the calculation.

When “variable annual recurring revenue” ramps over time, build a standard revenue model from historical monthly trends of new customers. Add this “estimated variable recurring revenue” to new customer CARR, and factor the estimated variable ARR gross margin into the calculation.

## Sample Calculations

### Sample Calculation #1: 90 Day Sales Cycle

#### List of Input Values

Sales and Marketing Expenses (Previous Quarter) = $1,000,000
New CARR (Current Quarter) = $600,000
Gross Margin = 80%

#### Calculation Formula

$$\frac{\$1{,}000{,}000}{\$600{,}000 \times 0.80} \times 12 = 25 \text{ Month CAC Payback Period}$$

### Sample Calculation #2: 30 Day Sales Cycle

#### List of Input Values

Sales and Marketing Expenses (Previous Month) = $500,000
New CARR (Current Month) = $750,000
Gross Margin = 86%

#### Calculation Formula

$$\frac{\$500{,}000}{\$750{,}000 \times 0.86} \times 12 = 9.3 \text{ Month CAC Payback Period}$$

### Sample Calculation #3: Usage-Based Pricing

#### List of Input Values

Sales and Marketing Expenses (Previous Quarter) = $600,000
New CARR (Current Quarter) = $300,000
Variable ARR (Current Quarter) = $200,000
Subscription Gross Margin = 80%
Variable ARR Gross Margin = 75%

#### Calculation Formula

$$\frac{\$600{,}000}{(\$300{,}000 \times 0.80) + (\$200{,}000 \times 0.75)} \times 12 = 18 \text{ Month CAC Payback Period}$$
